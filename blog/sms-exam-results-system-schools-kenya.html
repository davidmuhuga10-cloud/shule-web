Why SMS Still Beats WhatsApp for Reaching Every Parent

The parents a school most needs to reach are often the ones a WhatsApp group quietly leaves out.

It's tempting to assume every parent is reachable on WhatsApp — most staff members are on it constantly, so it feels universal from inside the school. It isn't. WhatsApp needs a smartphone and, more importantly, an active data bundle at the exact moment a message is sent. In many Kenyan households, that's true for one phone in the family, some of the time, not for every guardian, all the time.

What actually happens with a class WhatsApp group

A class WhatsApp group tends to reach the same reliably-connected 60–70% of parents every time, while the same handful of families miss every announcement — often the exact families a school most needs to stay in touch with about fees or a struggling grade. Group chats also bury an urgent fee reminder or exam result under twenty unrelated messages within minutes, and there's no way to confirm a specific parent actually saw it.

Why SMS is different

SMS works on the most basic phone in the market, needs no data bundle or Wi-Fi, arrives as its own message rather than buried in a group thread, and — sent through a proper SMS gateway — gives a delivery status per message, so a school can actually tell whether it reached the parent's phone. It's not a trendier technology than WhatsApp; it's simply the one built for universal reach rather than smartphone convenience, which is exactly the tradeoff that matters for a school communicating with every family, not just the ones with the newest phones.

What this looks like for exam results specifically

The moment exam results are ready is exactly when a school most needs every parent reached at once, reliably, without depending on who happened to open WhatsApp that day. A results-by-SMS system sends each parent their own child's marks directly and privately — not into a group where other parents can see it — deducting one SMS credit per message from a school's prepaid balance, with the whole exchange logged so a school can see exactly what was sent, to whom, and when.

The honest tradeoff

SMS isn't free the way a WhatsApp message is — each one costs a small, fixed amount, which is why schools using it prepay into a credit wallet rather than sending unlimited messages. But that cost buys something WhatsApp can't: certainty that the message reached a real phone, not just certainty that it was sent.
